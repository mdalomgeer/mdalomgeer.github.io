About

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Information Systems at the University of Maryland, Baltimore County, advised by Dr. Tera L. Reynolds in the CARE Research Lab. My research focuses on patient-centered AI: how people interact with large language models in high-stakes settings like healthcare, and how AI-generated responses differ from human communication in safety, empathy, personalization, and trust.

My work combines NLP, human-centered AI, and health informatics. I build and analyze datasets from online health communities, compare human responses against outputs from multiple LLMs, design annotation frameworks, and evaluate model behavior with computational metrics and human judgment. I work on grant-funded projects from Google (Society-Centered AI) and NIH/NIMHD. My team's cascaded LLM pipeline took 1st place in Question Interpretation at the NLM/NIH-organized ArchEHR-QA 2026 shared task.

My current focus: safety evaluation, empathy classification, and agent-based systems that improve AI responses in healthcare. I build AI that is technically strong and aligned with human needs in sensitive, real-world decisions.

TriAudit: Auditing and Improving Trustworthy LLMs for Health Consumers

Hussein MA, He L, Reynolds TL · Submitted to ICMLA, 2026

A closed-loop auditing pipeline that scores AI health answers for safety, empathy, and clinical sycophancy against anchored rubrics, then rewrites detected failures with a full audit trail. Applied to 1,199 responses from five LLMs, it reveals a safety, empathy, literacy, and sycophancy quadrilemma: no model excels on all four.

ICMLA 2026 Under review

Beyond the Visual Shortcut: Confound-Controlled Multimodal Drug-Sale Intent Detection on Instagram

Hosen MB, Rupa AP, Hussein MA · Submitted to ICMLA, 2026

Shows that reported gains from multimodal fusion are partly a labeling artifact. A confound-controlled 2×2 benchmark reveals the image channel alone collapses once the visual shortcut is removed, and cross-attention fusion is the only model reliable across all cells.
